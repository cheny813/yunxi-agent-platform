## 🏫 Campus Food Safety Regulations in China

### Core Legal & Regulatory Framework
- **Food Safety Law of the People’s Republic of China**:  
  - Article 34: Prohibits use of non-food substances, adulteration, and unsafe additives.  
  - Article 44: Mandates food operators to establish traceability systems and retain records for ≥6 months.  
  - Article 57: Imposes strict liability on schools (as “catering service providers”) for food safety incidents involving students.

- **Regulations on School Food Safety and Nutrition Health Management** (M.O.E. / SAMR / NHC Order No. 45, effective Apr 1, 2019):  
  - Establishes the *principal responsibility system*: school principals (or presidents) are legally accountable for canteen safety.  
  - Requires *meal accompaniment* (“dining with students”) by school leaders and teachers at least once per week.  
  - Bans nitrite-based food additives in school canteens.  
  - Restricts retail operations (e.g., snack bars) inside campus dining areas unless licensed and physically separated.  
  - Mandates food sampling: ≥125g per meal type, stored at 0–8°C for ≥48 hours.  
  - Requires real-time public disclosure via “Internet + Transparent Kitchen” (live video feeds of food prep areas).  
  - Specifies emergency reporting: suspected food safety incidents must be reported to local education and market regulation authorities within **2 hours**.

- **Catering Service Food Safety Operational Specifications** (SAMR Announcement No. 12, 2018):  
  - Provides granular operational standards for procurement, storage, processing, disinfection, waste management, and staff hygiene in institutional catering.

### Policy Updates & Implementation Guidance
- **MOE “Opinions on Strengthening Canteen Construction and Management in Primary and Middle Schools”** (2024):  
  - Emphasizes upgrading infrastructure (e.g., smart monitoring, cold-chain logistics), strengthening third-party auditing, and integrating nutrition health education into daily curriculum.  
  - Encourages provincial-level implementation guidelines—e.g., Guangdong and Zhejiang have issued detailed inspection scorecards and digital reporting platforms.

### Practical Support Tools
- A *Campus Food Safety Self-Inspection Checklist (Brief Version)* is available on demand—structured around Order No. 45’s 12 core obligations, designed for weekly internal audits by school administrators or safety officers.  
- Provincial/local rules (e.g., Shanghai Municipal Regulations on School Meal Services) and official interpretation documents can be retrieved upon request.
